### About the race
The 2026 Spanish Grand Prix takes place on 13 September at the Madring in Madrid, marking the first time the circuit has hosted the Spanish Grand Prix. The event is the fourteenth round of the 2026 Formula One World Championship and the 56th running of the Spanish Grand Prix as a round of the World Championship. The most recent venue to hold the Spanish Grand Prix was the Circuit de Barcelona-Catalunya at Montmeló, which now hosts the Barcelona-Catalunya Grand Prix on rotation with the Belgian Grand Prix.

### Championship standings
Mercedes hold a commanding lead in the Constructors' Championship with 468 points, well clear of Ferrari on 346 and McLaren on 287. Red Bull sit fourth on 204 points, with the RB F1 Team fifth on 75. In the Drivers' Championship, Andrea Kimi Antonelli leads on 267 points, with teammate George Russell second on 201 — a gap of 66 points between the two Mercedes drivers. Lewis Hamilton is third on 191 points, keeping the championship very much a Mercedes internal affair at the top. Lando Norris is fourth on 171 points and Charles Leclerc fifth on 155, meaning both Ferrari and McLaren still have realistic hopes of closing the gap over the remaining rounds.

### Previous race
At the Italian Grand Prix on 6 September 2026, run over 53 laps, Antonelli took victory ahead of teammate Russell, with Max Verstappen completing the podium in third. Lando Norris finished fourth and Oscar Piastri fifth, rounding out a strong result for McLaren. The result extended Antonelli's lead at the top of the Drivers' Championship and reinforced Mercedes' dominance in the Constructors' standings.

### Weather
Conditions at the Madring are sunny heading into the race weekend.

### Practice
Three free practice sessions were held across 11–12 September. Russell topped the opening session ahead of Antonelli and Leclerc. Antonelli then set the pace in the second session ahead of Leclerc and Hamilton, though the session was interrupted by a red flag after Arvid Lindblad of Racing Bulls hit the wall at turn 13. Antonelli continued his strong form by topping the third session ahead of Leclerc and Piastri. That session was red-flagged twice — first when Hamilton crashed into the wall at turn 22, and again in the closing stages when Oliver Bearman of Haas hit the wall at turn 10.
